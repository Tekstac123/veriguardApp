# AML Typology Handbook
*Deccan Commonwealth Bank - synthetic training data*

## 1. Structuring (Smurfing)

Structuring is the deliberate splitting of cash deposits or withdrawals into amounts below reporting or internal review thresholds. Indicators: repeated cash deposits just below Rs 10,00,000 or below internal thresholds; deposits spread across multiple branches or ATMs over a short period; round or near-round amounts; deposits followed by rapid transfer out.

## 2. Money Mule Accounts

A mule account receives funds from many unrelated parties and quickly moves them onward. Indicators: a high number of small inbound UPI or IMPS credits from distinct senders; outbound transfers within 24 hours of credit; account balance that repeatedly returns near zero; activity inconsistent with the declared occupation (for example, a student account with lakhs of rupees in monthly turnover).

## 3. Layering through High-Risk Jurisdictions

Layering moves funds through multiple accounts or jurisdictions to obscure their origin. Indicators: inbound SWIFT transfers from jurisdictions on the DCB High-Risk Jurisdiction Register, immediately split and sent to several domestic accounts; vague narrations such as 'consultancy' or 'advance'; no apparent business rationale.

## 4. Round-Tripping

Round-tripping moves funds out of an entity and back in to create the appearance of legitimate revenue or capital. Indicators: outbound payments to a counterparty followed by inbound receipts of similar value from a related party within 30 days; circular flows among entities with common directors.

## 5. Dormant Account Reactivation

An account inactive for 12 months or more that suddenly receives and disburses large sums is a risk indicator, particularly if contact details were changed shortly before reactivation.

## 6. Unexplained Wealth - PEP

Large credits to a PEP or a PEP's associate that do not match declared income or source of wealth require EDD and may warrant escalation.
